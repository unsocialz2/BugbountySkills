# Time and stopping decisions

Use an operator budget when provided. Otherwise make evidence-based checkpoints; do not invent elapsed time or impose universal hour limits.

At a checkpoint ask:
- Did the last experiment change a hypothesis, establish a control, or reveal a new boundary?
- Are failures caused by the hypothesis being wrong, or by missing access, broken tooling, expired sessions, or an invalid baseline?
- Is the next experiment discriminating, or just another encoding/payload with no new rationale?

Continue a productive path. Park a stagnant hypothesis with its evidence and a specific reopen condition. Mark missing prerequisites as blocked; do independent useful work while waiting. Pivot features or targets when remaining questions are less actionable than alternatives, rather than because an arbitrary timer expired.

Keep STATE concise and link to detailed experiments. Preserve enough actor/state/request context to resume without repeating work. Record actual duration only when measured. Reporting effort should match reproducibility and complexity; do not rush an incomplete report to meet a fixed schedule.
